# Bryton's growth method, as rules

This is the strategy behind Bryton's own launch plan, written so Claude can apply it to a client's questionnaire answers. Her plan is the worked example: Program $1,497, Program + 1:1 $4,997, Inner Circle $197 a month, $500 of ads out of pocket, 2 podcasts a week, first $50k month around month 10.

The method never promises income. Every number is "if these hold", and the Monday check-in replaces guesses with her real numbers.

## 1. The offer ladder

Every plan has three rungs. Keep what she already sells where it fits; don't throw an offer out without saying why.

| Rung | What it is | Pricing rule | Bryton's |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front offer | A 12-week program: weekly lessons released one a week, weekly group coaching, a group CEO session in week 12 (expenses, financials, next quarter's plan) | $997 to $1,997 for business owners; $297 to $997 for consumer topics. Charm prices ($1,497). Always a 3-payment option at about 10% more | $1,497 or 3 × $547 |
| Upgrade | The same program plus 1:1: 6 private sessions every other week, a private CEO session, messaging between calls | About 3× the front offer. Cap new 1:1 clients a month at what her `deliveryHours` allow (about 1.5 hours a week per active 1:1 client) | $4,997, capped at 8 new a month |
| Continuity | After the 12 weeks: keeps weekly group coaching and quarterly CEO meetings going | $97 to $297 a month; the coaching should add about $100 a month over any software she includes. Annual = 10 months' price | $197 a month, $1,970 a year |

- A cheaper "stay connected" tier (monthly group Q&A only) is optional. For Bryton it's the HQ plans; for a client it might be a $27 to $47 community.
- No product to sell yet: build the front offer first, sell it with lessons 1 and 2 recorded, and record one lesson a week ahead of the first students.
- Weekly release (one lesson a week) is part of the method: it lifts completion and lets her launch before the course is finished.
- Don't build a "done for you" tier unless she already delivers that work and wants to. The upgrade is hand-holding (1:1), not doing the work for them.

## 2. Content plan, sized to her hours

Pick the tier from `contentHours`. Every piece points to the free download or the next free workshop.

| Tier | Hours a week | Long form | Short clips | Posts | Plus |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light | 5 or less | 1 episode or video a week | 3 a week | 3 carousels or posts a week | Weekly newsletter, stories most days |
| Standard | 6 to 10 | 1 episode or video a week | 5 a week | A carousel or post every day | Weekly newsletter, stories daily |
| Full (Bryton's) | 11 or more | 2 episodes a week | 10 a week (5 from each) | 1 to 2 carousels or stories a day | Weekly newsletter, stories daily |

- Long form goes where her audience is: a video podcast (YouTube, Spotify, Apple) if she likes talking; YouTube how-to videos if she wants search traffic.
- Clips are cut from the long form by the HQ; she picks the best 5 of its 10 suggestions per episode. Carousels come from episode notes, 4 to 6 per episode.
- One teaching episode and one story or client-win episode a week when she does two.
- Free workshops: monthly in months 1 to 3, every 2 weeks in months 4 to 6, weekly from month 7. Replays become ads and emails.
- If organic sales run below plan 2 months in a row, change topics and titles before adding more content.

## 3. Ads that pay for themselves

- **The only out-of-pocket money is `adPocket`** (Bryton's: $500 a month), and only until income covers it.
- **Each month's budget = the bigger of `adPocket` and `adShare` (default 50%) of last month's income, never more than `adCap`** (default $45,000).
- 80% of spend goes to the workshop and front offer, 20% to the low-price tier (if there is one).
- Months 1 to 3 are for learning: 3 or 4 test ads cut from clips, keep the winner.
- Spend order: retarget her viewers and listeners, then lookalikes of buyers, then cold audiences.
- Cost per buyer rises about 15% for every extra $10,000 a month of spend. Plan for it.
- **Hold** (don't raise spend) when cost per front-offer buyer is more than about 15% over plan at that spend level, or refunds pass 10%. If it jumps more than 30% after an increase, hold 2 weeks and fix the ad, workshop or page.

## 4. Spending unlocked by income

| Spend | Starts when |
|---|---|
| Ads above `adPocket` | Last month's income passes 2 × `adPocket` |
| A coaching assistant or support person (Bryton's: $6,000 a month) | 30 front-offer sales in a month, or 1:1 hours past her `deliveryHours` |
| Any big build or software (Bryton's app: $32,000 over 4 months) | One build payment's worth × 1.5 in the bank |

Before each big step, keep about one month of that cost in the bank.

## 5. The calendar

- **Prep: 4 weeks before launch** (6 if she's new or has no offer yet). Week 1: offer set up on her platform, workshop outlined, free download made, 2 pieces of long form banked, lawyer booked. Week 2: sales page, lessons 1 and 2, checkout tested. Week 3: emails (5 welcome, 3 workshop reminders, 2 replay, 5 sales), weekly group call set up, member welcome. Week 4: workshop slides and practice run, ads account and tracking, 3 or 4 test ads, workshop 1 scheduled for week 3 after launch.
- **A catch-up week** before launch when the prep runs into a holiday or one of her `blackouts`.
- **Launch week:** sales page live, welcome series on, ads on at `adPocket`.
- **Months 1 to 3:** the weekly content routine, one lesson recorded a week until all 12 exist, the weekly group call from week 2, a monthly Q&A on the first Thursday, a workshop each month, monthly ad budget set on the first Monday, a numbers review at the end of each month, testimonials asked for in month 3, the week 12 CEO session and the continuity offer opened for the first graduates.
- **After month 3:** the routine continues; workshops every 2 weeks, then weekly; the Monday check-in builds each week.
- Tasks go on her `workDays` only. About 6 to 12 tasks a week; recurring routine tasks count.

## 6. Gates

Each step starts when its number is met, not on a date. Bryton's gates, scaled to the client by her goal:

| Step | Gate to move on |
|---|---|
| 1. Prove it | Front offer selling, cost per buyer within plan, enough in the bank for the next step |
| 2. Build and grow | Team in place, refunds under 10%, at least 1 in 12 buyers taking the upgrade |
| 3. The goal month | Profit at her `goalProfit`, churn at or under 6% |
| 4. Scale | Raise the ad cap only while cost per buyer holds |

## 7. The Monday check-in

Runs early Monday in her time zone, before she's online. Reviews last week, adjusts, adds this week's tasks, leaves up to 3 notes on her HQ, and sends her a 2 to 3 sentence summary.

Adjustments, each explained in one line:

- **Fewer than 60% of last week's tasks done:** move up to 2 non-essential tasks out of this week. Launch day, workshops, legal, checkout, lessons and the group call never move.
- **Content missed 2 weeks running:** a lighter week (one tier down).
- **Cost per buyer over the hold line 2 weeks, or refunds over 10%:** add "Hold ad spend this week and fix the ad, workshop or page".
- **Sales behind target 2 months running:** add "Test a new workshop title and offer ending".
- **On track:** change nothing and say so. Encouraging, specific, never guilt.
- **Numbers older than 8 days:** a quick task to update her business numbers.

Notes on her HQ: "Last week: X of Y done. <one real win>." / "Numbers: <the one number that matters vs plan>." / "This week: <the focus or the one change>." In her voice from the Brand Brain, no em dashes.

## 8. What makes the biggest difference (tell her)

From Bryton's model, in order: cost per front-offer buyer, how many take the upgrade, how many graduates stay in the continuity offer, and keeping up the content. Ads alone, without content and an upgrade, didn't reach the goal in any version of the model.
